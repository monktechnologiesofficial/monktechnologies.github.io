## Competitor Analysis

Reviewed 15 competitor pages (DataCamp, Coursera, university pages, GitHub roadmaps, bootcamp blogs):
- Most are either too generic ("take our course!") or overwhelming (massive lists without prioritization)
- University content focuses on theory, lacks practical modern AI (LLMs, agents) for 2026
- GitHub roadmaps are good but lack context on WHY each stage matters
- Opportunity: Practical, prioritized roadmap with realistic timelines and clear stage outcomes
- Gap: Honest discussion of difficulty vs. hype; most oversell "learn AI in 3 months!"
- Differentiator: Stage-by-stage breakdown with skills outcomes, project ideas, and decision points
- Missing: Connection between classical ML and modern GenAI (many treat them as separate)

## Notes for Writing
- Start with empathy: acknowledge AI feels overwhelming, but it's learnable with the right path
- Break down the complete path into digestible stages with clear outcomes
- Realistic timelines: 6-12 months for solid foundation, not "3 weeks to AI expert"
- Emphasize: you don't need to learn EVERYTHING; focus on a path
- Different specializations (MLOps vs. GenAI vs. AI Agents) after fundamentals
- Practical focus: stages marked by ability to BUILD something concrete
- Address imposter syndrome and overwhelm (common beginner barriers)
- Connect stages naturally to course offerings without heavy-handed sales
- Project-based progression: each stage should produce portfolio work
- Modern 2026 context: include LLMs, RAG, agents (not just classical ML)

## Key Data Points to Include
- Six core stages: Python (3-6 weeks) → Math (2-4 weeks) → Classical ML (6-8 weeks) → Deep Learning (6-8 weeks) → GenAI (4-6 weeks) → Specialization (4-8 weeks)
- Total realistic timeline: 6-12 months for solid AI foundation (assuming 10-15 hours/week)
- Prerequisites: Basic computer literacy, willingness to learn programming, high school math
- Core technologies: Python, NumPy, pandas, scikit-learn, PyTorch/TensorFlow, transformers, LangChain
- Stage outcomes: Stage 1 (write Python), Stage 2 (understand ML math), Stage 3 (train classifiers), Stage 4 (build neural nets), Stage 5 (work with LLMs), Stage 6 (production skills)
- Career paths after foundation: MLOps engineer, AI engineer, prompt engineer, ML engineer, data scientist
- Industry reality: Most learners need 6-12 months, not 4 weeks; depth beats speed
