## Competitor Analysis

Reviewed 11 competitor pages (Harvard Extension, Stanford online, bootcamp sites, career coaches):
- Most are either too generic (motivational only) or too technical (assume CS background)
- University pages focus on their programs; lack practical multi-path guidance
- Few address specific objections (age, no degree, no tech background)
- Opportunity: Honest, practical guide acknowledging different starting points
- Gap: Realistic timelines and specific project examples
- Differentiator: Clear paths based on existing background, not one-size-fits-all

## Search Intent Analysis

Primary searcher personas:
1. **Mid-career professionals (30-45)**: Established in non-tech field, see AI opportunity, want roadmap
2. **Early career pivots (25-30)**: 2-5 years in adjacent field, ready to specialize
3. **Late-career changers (45+)**: Experienced professionals adapting to market changes
4. **Adjacent tech workers**: Software engineers, data analysts wanting to move into AI
5. **Domain experts**: Nurses, lawyers, marketers wanting to add AI skills to expertise

Content should address: Realistic assessment of starting point, multiple viable paths, honest timelines, objections (age, education, background), concrete action steps, and how to choose training.

## Notes for Writing
- Start with empathy: acknowledge fear/uncertainty of career change
- Multiple paths, not one: different routes based on different starting points
- Honest about barriers: time, money, effort required
- Specific, actionable steps (not vague "learn AI")
- Address common objections directly (age, no CS degree, busy schedule)
- Include concrete project examples for portfolio building
- Realistic timelines: 6-18 months depending on starting point
- Connect domain expertise to AI opportunities (healthcare → AI health tech)
- Practical course selection advice (when bootcamp vs self-study vs university)
- Balance encouragement with realism
- Specific to 2026 market: what works now vs what worked in 2020
